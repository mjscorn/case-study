Case study

Exploring a redesign of the Customer Area through a critical communication case study.

The problem

Cracks, code switching, and comm control

Fragmented workflows

Workflows that span multiple job centers show the seams in platform products, waste customers' time in moving them over artificial paths, and erode trust.

Navigation built for everyone

Navigation in products that serve multiple personas and JTBDs at their worst reflect internal team structures and incentives; at their best, they reflect customers' mental models without overwhelming.

No cohesive comms strategy

Notifications and messaging that behave at random give customers a sense that no one is in control and a "boy who cried wolf" dismal of critical comms.

Process

Falling in love with the problem

Gather

Data and customer insight. Start with deeply understanding the root, not the wilting pedals.

Analyze

Industry trends and competitive analysis across the similar industries, as well as tech advances that.

Explore

Without constraints, within a two-to-three-year vision and then work backwards to the MVP.

The experience solve

Orchestration,
not conversation.

A redesign of Adyen's Customer Area where the assistant doesn't chat about problems — it resolves them, asks permission once, and turns the fix into a reusable skill.

The shift

From  →  to

A dashboard you read→A workspace that acts
Notifications as a list→Notifications as cases with options
Chat as a destination→Chat as one layer over the work
One-off fixes→Fixes that become skills
Trust via polish→Trust via visibility — cards, plans, permission
Today

What’s fading

SparklesThe era of AI as a labeled destination is ending.
Alert fatigueA bell full of undifferentiated red dots. Severity without a next step is just noise.
Chat as the whole productBreaks flow and taxes users with re-explaining context. Chat survives as one layer and not the surface everything passes through.
Settings-page automationRules buried in configuration. Automation should be offered at the moment of a repeated fix, not hunted for.
Tomorrow

What's rising

Embedded resolution

The assistant lives beside the work, pulls the case up with its evidence, and acts — the dashboard stays the source of truth.

Permission surfaces

One structured ask at the moment of a repeated fix: allow always, ask each time, or just notify. Consent is a first-class UI object.

Skills over settings

A resolved case compresses into a named, reusable skill — created in the flow, visible in the rail, editable later.

Calm, secondary AI

Collapsible panels and contextual cards that appear only when they add value. The metric is resolution, not engagement.

The resolve flow

Surface · resolve · automate

Surface

A critical email lands in the inbox with the story already assembled — customer, amount, refusal, retry — and one door into the Customer Area.

Resolve

The case opens knowing what happened. Options carry keyboard shortcuts, the chat bar continues the flow, and every action reports back as a card.

Automate

After the fix works once, the assistant asks to own it next time. A single yes becomes a skill with a name and a badge.

surface → resolve → automateThe lifecycle of a notification, compressed into one thread.
